# Conditional single-cohort label-allocation diagnostic

2026-09-11, after the joint 200-allocation results and rank-implementation sensitivities were known, before inspecting the conditional distributions below. This is an explanatory diagnostic, not a new confirmatory analysis, and does not revise the original Holm families or select a new endpoint to seek a positive result.

Reuse all saved allocation-level statistics from null_fixed_common_all_allocations.tsv.gz and null_dynamic_gate_all_allocations.tsv.gz. For each original cell selection and each previously reported metric/statistic, retain (a) the 20 discovery allocations with the validation labels fixed at their observed configuration and (b) the 10 validation allocations with the discovery labels fixed as observed. Preserve the original fixed candidate sets, numerical-zero convention, and the already-recomputed allocation-specific dynamic 10% eligibility sets. No scores, cells, genes or candidate edges are resampled, and no additional joint allocations are generated.

For each subset, calculate its exact one-sided upper-tail fraction, including the observed allocation, with the original 10^-12 comparison tolerance. Export the full conditional distributions, observed statistic, mean/median, 2.5th/97.5th null-reference percentiles and candidate-denominator range. Report unadjusted diagnostic P values and their 20- or 10-allocation resolution; no new claim of multiplicity-adjusted confirmation is made. The original families A (30), B (24) and C (6) and their results remain unchanged. The gap statistics from family B are not part of this diagnostic request.

Interpretation: joint label permutation addresses the assumption that condition labels have no association within either study, with both label assignments randomized. Rejecting that global no-association configuration is insufficient to establish reproducible condition association in each study separately. Fixing the other cohort's observed labels shows how much tail evidence remains when only one cohort is randomized. A replication claim would require a separately justified analysis of the union null that at least one cohort lacks the relevant association. Do not present a maximum of the conditional P values as a newly validated confirmatory replication test. The two-case validation design and its conditional allocation granularity remain substantive limits.
